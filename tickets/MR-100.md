### BA → client · 2026-10-27
Thanks for the brief. Before we write the spec, we need your decisions on these. Each
one changes what we build. Please answer under each question; "not sure yet" is a fine
answer, and we'll plan around it safely. Background for each is in
`intent/persona-conversation.md`.

**Q1. Her written style only, or her voice too?**
Should the persona match the way she wrote and spoke in words, or also sound like her?
Why it matters: cloning a voice is a separate system with its own consent and misuse risks.
> Answer (Lead, 2026-10-28): Words now, voice later. For this round the persona matches
> how she wrote and spoke in words: her phrasing, nicknames and tone, taken from her
> stories, transcripts and quotes. Sounding like her comes later as a separate brief,
> with its own consent rules.

**Q2. Who is "Mom" in the data?**
Today each memory just carries a typed name for the person. Is a persona one named
person per memorial, and who decides that "Mom" and "Mẹ" are the same person?
Why it matters: we may need a record for the person before anything else.
> Answer (Lead, 2026-10-28): A person record. Each person the family remembers gets one
> record, and each persona belongs to one person record. The family member who built
> the memorial links memories to it and decides that “Mom” and “Mẹ” are the same
> person. The AI may suggest a match, but a person always confirms it.

**Q3. Who may talk with the persona?**
Only the family member who built the memorial, or others they invite? Who can take
access away again?
Why it matters: today memories are private to one account, so sharing is new.
> Answer (Lead, 2026-10-28): Invited family members. The family member who built the
> memorial owns the person record and invites others to it. Invited members can talk
> with the persona, but they can't edit memories or invite anyone else. Only the owner
> can take access away, and doing so ends any chat that member has open. Children are
> covered in Q4.

**Q4. Can grandchildren under 18 use it, and with whose consent?**
Why it matters: safety and consent rules for children.
> Answer (Lead, 2026-10-28): Adults only in v1. Only members 18 or older can be invited
> this round. Children come later as a separate brief, designed with a parent or
> guardian's consent and checked against the privacy law where we operate.

**Q5. What should it say when the memories don't cover a question?**
Why it matters: without a fixed reply, the AI tends to invent an answer.
> Answer (Lead, 2026-10-28): A fixed, gentle reply, the same every time: it says plainly
> that there is no memory of that, and invites the family member to add the story if
> they remember it. Example: “I don't have a memory of that. If you remember it, add it
> to her stories and I can talk about it.” It cites nothing, and when no memory matches,
> the AI isn't asked to write anything.

**Q6. What does "remember what we talked about last time" mean?**
The full chat or a summary? Per family member or shared? For how long, and can a family
member delete it? Should anything said in a chat ever become a memory?
Why it matters: privacy, deletion, and whether chats can change the record of her life.
> Answer (Lead, 2026-10-28): A short summary, not the full chat. Each family member has
> their own summary, which only they can see; the owner can't read other members' chats.
> It is kept until that member deletes it, and it is deleted when the owner removes
> their access. Nothing said in a chat becomes a memory on its own: a member can offer
> it as a new memory, and the owner reviews it like any other.

**Q7. Does it ever speak as her ("I remember…") or claim to be her?**
Should it always be clear that it's an AI recreation?
Why it matters: honesty with grieving families.
> Answer (Lead, 2026-10-28): It may speak in her way, in the first person (“I
> remember…”), because that is what makes it feel like her. It never claims to be her.
> Every conversation opens with a short notice that this is an AI recreation built from
> the family's memories, and if someone asks “are you really Mom?” it says so plainly.

**Q8. Everything the AI found, or only what the family confirmed?**
Today every item the AI extracted can be used unless someone rejected it. Should the
persona use only items a family member has confirmed?
Why it matters: there's no "confirmed" step today; adding one changes what it knows.
> Answer (Lead, 2026-10-28): Only what the family confirmed. The persona uses an item
> only after a family member has confirmed it or corrected it. Items the AI found but
> nobody has checked yet are not used. This adds a confirm step; search can keep working
> as it does today.

**Q9. When is the next demo, and what must it show?**
A working backend API, or a conversation people can click through? (App screens are out
of scope this round.)
Why it matters: it decides what we slice into the first tickets.
> Answer (Lead, 2026-10-28): The demo is on 2026-11-11 (Lead to confirm the date). It
> must show a working backend API, called from the API docs or a script: open a
> conversation, ask three questions on synthetic family data, and show one grounded
> reply with sources, one “no memory of that” reply, and a second conversation that
> recalls the first. No app screens.

**Q10. How fast must a reply feel?**
The AI runs on our own machine, which is slow without a GPU.
Why it matters: it decides streaming, model size and hardware, without moving family
data to an outside AI service.
> Answer (Lead, 2026-10-28): The first words should appear within about 3 seconds, so
> replies stream. A full reply should finish within about 30 seconds on the demo
> machine. If we can't hit that on CPU, we use a smaller local model or a local GPU,
> never an outside AI service.

**Q11. How will families judge that it "feels like talking to Mom"?**
Why it matters: we need a measurable outcome before we can test it.
> Answer (Lead, 2026-10-28): Two family members who knew her rate 20 replies on
> synthetic data, without knowing which version wrote them: “sounds like her” and “true
> to her memories”, each 1 to 5. Target: an average of 4 or more on both, and zero
> replies that invent a memory. After launch, we also count how often families flag
> “that isn't how she talked”.

**Q12. Do you want screens for talking with the persona?** *(BA, 2026-10-29)*
In Q9 you said the demo is API only and screens are out of scope this round. The backend
(conversation, sources, summary, invites, offers) is now built, but there is no screen for any
of it: today the persona can only be used from the API docs or a script. Only the people page
(MR-101) and the confirm buttons (MR-102) have screens.
Why it matters: without screens no family member can talk with it, and a screen that handles
the AI-recreation notice, sources and sharing wrongly would undo the safeguards in Q3, Q4, Q6 and Q7.
Proposed, if yes (tickets written, all blocked until you answer, not in the 2026-11-11 demo):
MR-110 chat window, MR-111 "what we talked about" panel, MR-112 list of persons shared with me
(small API), MR-113 invite, list and revoke screens, MR-114 offer-a-memory screens.
Please answer: yes or no, whether any of it should be ready for the demo, and which comes first.
> Answer: _pending_
