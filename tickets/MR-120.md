### BA → client · 2026-11-04
Thanks for the brief. Each question below changes what we build or what we promise
families. Please answer under each one; "not sure yet" is fine and we will plan around it
with the safe default shown. Background is in `intent/mcp-server.md`.

**Q1. What is the MCP client for, and who uses it?**
Is it the robot itself, a desktop assistant, a developer tool, or something else? Which
family members would use it, and is there a demo date?
Why it matters: it decides how a client signs in, which tools matter first, and what "worked" means.
Safe default until decided: the same accounts and rights as the app (owner and invited members), no demo date.

**Q2. Do the tools see what the app's two paths see, or only the persona's?**
Today `POST /search/ask` answers from every item that is not rejected, while the persona
conversation uses only items a family member confirmed or corrected (intent Q8 of
persona-conversation). Should `ask_memory` and `search_memories` use the same
confirmed-only rule?
Why it matters: unconfirmed AI findings could otherwise reach an assistant that speaks about the person.
Safe default: confirmed items only, scoped to one person.

**Q3. Should `ask_memory` answer like the neutral helper or in her voice?**
The helper writes about her in the third person. The persona may speak in the first person
and opens with an AI-recreation notice.
Why it matters: a first-person reply passed on by another assistant could be mistaken for her words.
Safe default: the neutral third-person helper, no persona voice, through MCP.

**Q4. What does `add_memory` create: a memory, or an offer for the owner to review?**
A direct memory is saved as written and goes through extraction. An offer waits for the
owner to accept (the app already has this for invited members). The persona brief said
chats never become memories without the owner's review.
Why it matters: text written by an AI assistant could enter the record of her life unreviewed.
Safe default: an offer for the owner to review; only an owner may create a memory directly, and only after confirmation (Q5).

**Q5. What does "needs confirmation" mean for `add_memory`?**
MCP lets a client ask the person to approve a tool call, but the server cannot force it.
Is client-side approval enough, or must the server itself require a second step (for
example a returned confirmation token, or the owner reviewing in the app)?
Why it matters: without a server-side step, a misbehaving client could write without anyone approving it.
Safe default: the server requires a second step; approval in the client alone is not trusted.

**Q6. How is a client counted as local, and how does it prove who the user is?**
The server cannot see which model the client uses. Do we only listen on this machine
(for example a local process the client starts), and sign in with the person's own app
account? Or is there a list of approved clients?
Why it matters: "no memory text leaves the machine" cannot be checked from the server side alone.
Safe default: local connection only, never reachable from the network, the person's own account, and a note in the docs that the client must use a local model.

**Q7. May `search_memories` return the raw text of memories to the client?**
Answers cite memories, but a search returns the stories themselves, which is more sensitive.
Why it matters: raw text goes straight into another program's context.
Safe default: return it, capped at 20 hits as the app does, only for one person the caller may read; nothing is logged.

**Q8. May a superuser reach everyone's memories through MCP?**
The app lets a superuser search all users with `all_users`.
Why it matters: an assistant acting for an administrator could read every family's memories.
Safe default: no; MCP never offers `all_users`.
